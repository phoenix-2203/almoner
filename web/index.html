01

How it works

1

A person signs the rules

Purpose, allowed categories, blocked terms, sellers, budget, per-purchase cap and an approval line. One task, one mandate.

2

The vault issues the agent's key

A NEAR function-call key on the vault's own account. It can call three methods. It can't transfer, add keys or call anything else.

3

Sellers sign every price

The agent forwards a signed quote. The vault checks the signature on-chain, so nobody can edit the price, the item or the payee.

4

Escrow, then proof

Funds wait until the seller delivers. Accepted work is paid. Junk or silence is refunded and the seller goes on hold.

04

Questions a careful reader asks

Isn't this just a spending limit?

No. A limit answers "can the agent afford this?". Almoner also answers "is this what it was asked to buy, at the price the seller signed, and did the work arrive?". Purchases inside the budget are still refused when they don't serve the task.

What can the published key actually do?

Three things: ask the vault to buy, accept or reject a delivery for its own task, and settle a purchase whose time window has passed. It can also burn its own gas allowance of 1 NEAR, after which it stops working. It can't transfer funds, add keys or call any other contract. NEAR enforces that, not our code.

Why does this need NEAR?

NEAR accounts can hold several keys with different powers, and a contract can add and delete keys on its own account. That lets the vault hand an agent a key that is limited by the protocol itself. Seller signatures are checked with a built-in host function, and payments use the NEP-141 token standard.

What doesn't it protect against?

IntentLock is rule-based: it checks categories and blocked terms, not meaning. A seller can mislabel an item, at the risk of rejection, a refund and a hold. Delivery quality is judged by the buyer. The owner is trusted to set sensible rules. The full list is in the README.

Is it ready for real money?

No. It runs on NEAR testnet with a test token, and the contract hasn't been audited. It was built in a few days for NEAR HACKATHON, with 31 contract tests and the public challenge above.

See it refuse you.

Open the product, press a button, and watch a real transaction signed with the agent's key bounce off the vault. Every attempt shows up in the public record a few seconds later.
